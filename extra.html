Welcome to the story of my life.

Hi, I'm Rana Erbas

I was born on September 9, 2004, at the Jessa Hospital in Hasselt. At the time, my parents were living in Genk, but my mother’s gynecologist was based in Hasselt, which is why I was born there. I am the youngest in our family, with an older brother and sister. I was actually a bit of a surprise, my parents were not planning to have any more children. In fact, just as they were considering procedures to prevent further pregnancies, my mother discovered she was already 13 weeks pregnant with me. And here I am!

Our biggest step!

At the time of my birth, we were living in Kolderbos, a neighborhood in Genk. Over the years, we moved a total of five times, but always within Genk. These moves were largely due to financial struggles, as both my parents were still studying and working part-time student jobs to make ends meet.

When I was in the second grade, we finally moved into our first owned home. It was a big step for our family, as we had always lived in rented houses before. However, this move came with a tough decision, my siblings and I had to change schools. It wasn’t easy to leave behind familiar faces and routines, but it marked the beginning of a more stable chapter for our family.

The journey of our biggest step.

The first few months in our new home were perfect, a time when everything seemed to be falling into place. But then my biological father lost his job. The stress of this loss weighed heavily on him, and it began to manifest in harmful ways. His frustration and anger turned into physical aggression, which he directed toward me.

From the age of 8 until I was 15, I stayed silent about what was happening. I didn’t tell anyone, not teachers, not friends, not even extended family. The only people who knew were my siblings, but they were too scared to speak up as well.

We ended up living in that house for five years. During that time, the violence continued. It became part of the hidden reality we lived with, a secret that we carried in silence. Eventually, circumstances forced another change. We moved out of the house and went to live with my biological father’s parents.

The downfall of my life.

After moving in with my grandparents, our struggles only seemed to deepen. Because my siblings were starting secondary school, I had to change schools once again. They both enrolled at SJB College in Genk, which meant I was sent to De Bret in Genk, right across from their school.

We ended up living with my grandparents for five years. It was during this time that I experienced what true fatherly love felt like, but not from my biological father. It came from my grandfather. He was patient, kind, and supportive in ways I had never known. While my biological father remained distant and unchanged, my grandfather stepped into a role that I hadn’t realized I was missing so deeply.

Those years were a mixture of pain and healing. Living under one roof with my grandparents brought its own challenges, but the love and care I received from my grandfather became a small light in a dark time.

The downfall of our new life/home.

When we moved in with my grandparents, my parents purchased a plot of land in Houthalen. This marked the first time we would move away from Genk. The plan was ambitious, building a house from the ground up. My biological father insisted on doing most of the work himself, which meant the process was slow and stretched over three long years.

Finally, on January 3, 2020, we moved into our new home as a family: my siblings, my parents, and I. It was a milestone for all of us, representing a fresh start in a new place. The journey to get there had been anything but easy, but stepping into that house felt like the beginning of a new chapter for our family.

The end of a 20-year marriage.

We moved into our new home as a family: my parents, my siblings, and our cat. The house was a duplex, divided into four separate units. The plan was for us to live in the top unit while renting out the other three to generate extra income.

or the first two months, everything seemed perfect. But during the period of the Islamic fast, my biological father suddenly began acting erratically. His outbursts became more frequent, and then, one day, he left our home without much explanation. We later learned the truth: his departure was just a cover for the fact that he had been unfaithful. His mistress was pregnant, and this marked the end of their secret relationship and the beginning of our family's separation.

Not long after, my parents divorced, finally! For the first time in years, it felt like a weight had been lifted. From that point on, it was just us: my siblings, my mother, and our cat. We stayed together, leaning on one another to rebuild a sense of peace and stability in our lives.

Now, here we are in the present.

We’ve been living in this house for five years now, but we’re planning to move again soon. Since the divorce, we’ve had no contact with our biological father, and that chapter of our lives feels firmly closed.

This year, on March 2, 2024, we suffered a devastating loss: the passing of our grandfather. He was the man we always considered to be our real father, and his loss has left a void in our hearts.

Our family has grown in a small way since then, we now have three cats in total. They bring comfort and joy to our home.

Both of my siblings are still studying. My brother is in the final year of his master’s degree in Business Administration, which he extended to finish his thesis. My sister is also working toward her master’s degree in Physiotherapy, with 1.5 years left to go.

As for me, I’m currently studying for an associate degree in DVO at PXL. If all goes as planned, I’ll graduate at the same time as my sister, which feels like a fitting milestone for our family to celebrate together.
